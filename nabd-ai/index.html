Part I Architecture Specification

1 Executive Summary & Core Objectives

NABD AI is engineered as an institutional governance, validation and decision-control layer that decouples application logic from raw foundation models. Designed specifically for highly regulated environments, it enforces rigorous safeguards against autonomous execution risk, algorithmic hallucination and unchecked decision-making. The system produces evidence, not verdicts; the authority to act remains, at every point, with a named human being.

Controlled Decision-Support

Facilitates policy analysis, regulatory compliance extraction, procurement review and operational planning through structured advisory outputs.

Absolute Segregation of Authority

Operates strictly under draft-only, read-only or advisory parameters. No production writes, transactional approvals or external communications without authorized human execution.

Model-Agnostic Infrastructure

Complete independence from foundation model providers. Queries route through interchangeable models (Jais or approved enterprise LLMs) while ownership of the evaluation and governance pipeline is retained.

Deterministic Governance Enforcement

Programmed rule boundaries classify incoming and outgoing data, manage uncertainty, and immediately isolate unverified or high-risk outcomes.

2 System Topology & Architectural Division

The system is architecturally split into two interdependent layers. Layer A never speaks directly to a client application; Layer B never speaks directly to a foundation model.

Rule-based gatekeeper parsing both user inputs and model outputs against fixed organizational rules. Scores uncertainty, flags missing compliance fields, checks delegation-of-authority matrices and blocks prohibited operations.

3 Advanced Methodologies & Pipeline Architecture

3.1 Multi-Stage Refinement & Model Chaining Protocol

To eliminate single-model hallucinations and inaccurate probabilistic predictions, complex queries are routed through a sequential multi-model pipeline. The pipeline is bounded: there is exactly one refinement pass, and no stage may loop back on itself.

  1. Stage 1

    Initial Extraction & Drafting — Generator Model

    Ingests the query alongside authorized context chunks retrieved via Controlled RAG. Produces an initial draft or structured data extraction.

  2. Stage 2

    Policy & Fact Verification — Critic / Verifier Model

    Performs an adversarial review of Stage 1 output. Cross-references every claim, date, monetary figure and reference against the raw source text to identify discrepancies, missing mandates or overconfident extrapolations.

  3. Stage 3

    Synthesis & Governance Formatting — Refiner Model

    Incorporates Stage 2 feedback, rectifies errors, calculates remaining ambiguity and compiles the standardized Decision Readiness Packet.

Section 8 amends this protocol. Stage 3 is restructured so that it can add detail but cannot revise the decision itself. See 8.3 Locked Coarse Decision.

3.2 Decision Readiness Packet Specification

The system does not return raw conversational prose for material operations. It generates a structured review dossier containing the following metadata:

  • Problem Context & Intended Outcome — summary of the operational request and affected stakeholders.
  • Source Lineage & Evidence — direct references, document IDs and exact excerpts from approved policies.
  • Uncertainty & Risk Assessment — numerical and categorical indicators of model certainty, edge cases and potential policy conflicts.
  • Recommended Route — assigned to one of three strictly governed pathways.

Ready within defined controls

Full policy compliance verified, low residual risk, ready for human sign-off.

Human review required

Edge case identified, high uncertainty, or policy ambiguity detected.

Cannot proceed under current conditions

Missing critical documentation, violated baseline policy, or out-of-scope request.

6 High-Impact Domains & Mandatory Exclusions V1 Scope Boundary

6.1 Mandatory Human Decision Gates

The following workflows legally prohibit autonomous execution and require explicit, recorded human approval:

  • Employment actions — hiring, termination, promotion, performance rating.
  • Credit, financing, exemption or subsidy approvals.
  • Allocation of public benefits, housing or specialized service tiers.
  • Enforcement, fines, sanctioning and fraud allegations.
  • Critical IT system shutdown or account deactivation impacting operational continuity.
  • Vendor tender awarding and binding legal or contractual commitments.

6.2 Strict Scope Exclusions

To preserve clinical safety and regulatory alignment, NABD AI V1 strictly excludes the following capabilities:

  • Clinical diagnosis, prognosis, treatment planning or triage recommendations.
  • Medication calculation, dosing suggestions or prescription generation.
  • Real-time patient monitoring and physiological alert management.
  • Medical imaging, radiology, pathology or DICOM data analysis.
  • Medical device software integration (SaMD) or surgical robotics interfaces.
  • Autonomous agent swarms with self-delegating execution loops.
  • Direct write access to electronic health records or primary production databases.
  • Biometric surveillance, real-time facial recognition or emotional tracking.
  • Any workflow evaluated with a High or Very High residual risk profile.

7.1 Agent Security & Design Literature

Two further sources inform the defensive posture of the architecture. AgentDojo is a benchmarking framework built to test how well agents resist prompt injection while performing multi-step tasks across diverse environments; its central finding is that even advanced models struggle to hold both security and utility once untrusted data flows through external tools. Anthropic's guidance on building effective agents distinguishes rigid programmatic workflows from flexible autonomous agents and argues for simplicity and transparency, favouring modular designs such as prompt chaining and parallelization to improve reliability.

Read together, they set the two constraints NABD AI is built around: untrusted content must never reach a stage that holds authority, and the pipeline must stay a legible, bounded workflow rather than an open-ended agent. Both are load-bearing in Section 8.

8 Hierarchical Coarse-to-Fine Decision Synthesis Amends §3.1

A hierarchical prediction technique from sequence modelling maps unusually cleanly onto the Multi-Stage Refinement Pipeline. Adopting it converts three of NABD AI's governance properties from instructions a model is asked to follow into structural constraints a model cannot violate, and it does so inside the existing 576-hour envelope.

8.1 The Source Pattern

i

Hierarchical tokenization

Continuous data is compressed by an encoder-quantizer-decoder into a discrete code that is split into a coarse subtoken capturing dominant structure and a fine subtoken capturing only residual detail. The split is trained jointly, with a loss that forces the hierarchy rather than merely suggesting it.

ii

Two-step autoregression

The coarse subtoken is predicted first from history. The fine subtoken is predicted second, conditioned on both the history and the coarse prediction via cross-attention. One hard prediction becomes two easier, sequential ones.

iii

Exposure-bias correction

During training the fine stage conditions on the model's own predicted coarse output rather than ground truth. This closes the train/inference gap, because ground truth is never available at real inference time.

What is not being imported. NABD AI trains no quantizer and learns no codebook. What transfers is the discipline: a hard split between structure and detail, a fixed conditioning order, and an evaluation regime that feeds each stage the previous stage's real output. The mechanism is applied to an existing three-stage LLM pipeline, not to a learned tokenizer.

8.2 The Decision Token, Split

A Decision Readiness Packet is treated as one decision token with two parts. Everything that carries governance authority is coarse; everything else is fine.

Coarse subtoken

Governance-material structure

  • Recommended Route — one of the three pathways in §3.2.
  • Risk band — Low, Moderate, High, Critical, Unknown.
  • Output class — the CRS archetype of §4.
  • Reviewer requirement — seniority and review depth.
  • Blocking-gate flags — any §6.1 gate or §6.2 exclusion hit.

Small, closed, enumerable. Cheap to constrain at decode time, cheap to verify, cheap to diff between stages.

Fine subtoken

Residual detail

  • Citation spans — document IDs, page and line offsets.
  • Risk sub-scores — per-factor values inside the fixed band.
  • Rationale prose — wording of findings and caveats.
  • Edge-case notes — ambiguities and conflicts observed.
  • Presentation — packet formatting and ordering.

Open-ended text, which is where hallucination lives. It is never permitted to be load-bearing for authority.

8.3 Locked Coarse Decision — Generator / Refiner Restructuring

Under §3.1 as originally written, the Refiner receives verifier feedback and recompiles the whole packet. Nothing structural stops it from quietly upgrading Human review required to Ready within defined controls while polishing prose. The restructured sequence removes that degree of freedom.

  1. 0
    Deterministic pre-pass. The Dominant Factor Scoring Algorithm (2.2.3) computes the risk band in code from the factor matrix and evidence completeness. No model has run yet.
  2. 1
    Generator emits the coarse block first. Under constrained decoding restricted to the permitted enumerations, before it writes a single sentence of draft prose.
  3. 2
    Verifier checks claims word-for-word against the frozen evidence manifest, as before.
  4. 3
    Refiner receives the coarse block as read-only context and may emit fine detail only, conditioned on it. It cross-attends to the locked decision; it cannot rewrite it.

Invariant. sha256(coarse_block) must be byte-identical before and after Stage 3. A mismatch is not treated as a correction. It is a contradiction, and it trips the Contradiction Stop Trigger (2.3.2), routing the case to MORE_EVIDENCE or BLOCKED. Hallucination-by-rewrite stops being a behaviour to discourage and becomes a packet that will not serialize.

8.4 Risk Band Monotonicity — Scorer Hardening

The same factorization applies to risk. The coarse band is computed deterministically first; the model may only work inside it.

Operation on the risk band Permitted? Handling
Refine within band Permitted The fine layer may sharpen a value inside its band — High becomes High (3.7 / 5). The band label is unchanged.
Cross a band downward Prohibited Structurally rejected at serialization. The model has no path by which it can lower assessed risk.
Cross a band upward Escalated Not applied by the model. Emitted as an escalation signal to the deterministic engine, which re-runs the dominant-factor rule against the new evidence and may itself raise the band.

The result is monotonic in the fail-closed direction: risk can only ever be confirmed or raised, and raising it still requires deterministic agreement. The dominant-factor rule stops being a guideline the scorer is expected to respect and becomes the only writer of the band.

8.5 Exposure-Bias-Aligned TEVV

The exposure-bias lesson is a testing instruction. A verifier and refiner validated against hand-written, clean drafts are measured on a distribution that never occurs in production, because in production they only ever see the Generator's real, imperfect output.

Stage 2 and Stage 3 are therefore evaluated against a versioned corpus of captured Stage 1 outputs with their failure modes preserved:

  • Fabricated or subtly mis-attributed citation spans.
  • Mis-parsed dates, monetary figures and effective-date ranges.
  • Overconfident extrapolation beyond what the retrieved bylaw states.
  • Silently dropped mandates and missing compliance fields.
  • Injected instructions that survived retrieval and reached the draft.

The corpus is a controlled artifact: it is versioned alongside the pipeline and grows with every red-team finding, so a regression in Stage 2 or Stage 3 against a previously observed Stage 1 failure is a build failure. This is the direct operational answer to AgentDojo's finding that agents degrade sharply once untrusted tool output enters the loop — that degradation is measured here rather than assumed absent.

8.6 Cost & Latency Consequences

Two small constrained calls are cheaper and more controllable than one large unconstrained generation, which makes this restructuring a direct contributor to the existing optimization task rather than a tax on it.

Dimension Single unconstrained generation Coarse call, then fine call
Tokens on the authority-bearing output Hundreds of prose tokens, any of which may carry the decision. A handful of enum tokens under constrained decode.
Model tier required Frontier-tier for the entire packet. A small, cheap model can serve the coarse stage; spend is concentrated on fine detail.
Cost of verifying the route Parse prose to discover what was decided. Read one field and compare one hash.
Caching and replay The packet is one indivisible artifact. The coarse block is independently cacheable and replayable, supporting 4.2.4.
Blast radius of a bad generation One poor generation corrupts the decision. A poor fine pass degrades wording; the decision is untouched.
